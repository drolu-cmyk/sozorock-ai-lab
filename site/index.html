Why the Lab exists

Access to AI is growing. Practical capability is not evenly distributed.

AI inclusion is not achieved by giving people another tool. It requires the ability to use AI meaningfully, improve work, protect sensitive information, retain human judgment, and participate economically. The Lab turns that purpose into applied learning and implementation.

Individuals and workers

Build capability

Develop practical skills around real work, career needs, and community problems.

Small businesses

Improve operations

Design useful systems without needing a large internal technical team.

Community institutions

Strengthen delivery

Use AI responsibly to improve administration, knowledge, and service coordination.

Public and education partners

Expand participation

Support workforce readiness, responsible adoption, and local institutional capacity.

The Lab method

Start with the work, not the tool.

Each participant begins with a real task, workflow, handoff, or service problem. Tools are selected only after the work, users, data, risks, and expected result are understood.

01

Understand

Learn how AI works, where it fails, and what responsible use requires.

02

Map

Document the work, decisions, bottlenecks, data, and people affected.

03

Build

Create a usable system around a defined need rather than a tool demonstration.

04

Test

Use controlled scenarios and real work to identify failures and improve the design.

05

Deploy

Place the system into an appropriate setting with clear human responsibility.

06

Govern

Document data boundaries, oversight, risks, limitations, changes, and escalation.

Work from the Lab

Real needs. Working systems. Documented learning.

Projects are developed with participant consent and published carefully. The aim is not to inflate results. It is to show the original problem, the system created, what remained under human control, and what changed after use.

Small-business operations

Administrative workflow support

Exploring how AI can support estimates, documentation, scheduling, follow-up, and recurring administrative work for a service business.

Property operations

Recurring responsibility system

Organizing tasks, property information, scheduling, rent-related records, and recurring responsibilities into a clearer operating workflow.

Cloud and security foundations

Hands-on technical practice

Moving from introductory cloud concepts into structured data, storage, access decisions, and practical cybersecurity awareness.

What counts as progress

Measure the change, not the attendance.

The Lab records the condition before the project, the system built, the safeguards applied, and the result after controlled or real use.

Before

Define the friction

What was slow, repeated, missed, expensive, difficult to retrieve, or dependent on one person?

Build

Document the system

What was created, how it works, where AI is used, and where people remain responsible.

After

Record the result

What improved, what failed, what risk remained, and what should happen next?

Responsible implementation

Useful systems need boundaries.

Governance is part of the build process, not an afterthought. Every project should be able to explain what data may be used, what the system should not do, how outputs are checked, and who is accountable.

Project safeguards

  • Data and privacy boundaries
  • Human review and decision ownership
  • Reliability and failure testing
  • Security and access controls
  • Bias, exclusion, and accessibility review
  • Version and change documentation

Deployment record

  • Problem and intended users
  • Approved and prohibited uses
  • Known limitations
  • Escalation and incident procedure
  • Evidence of testing
  • Review date and next decision

Program information

Applied learning built around a real project.

Applicants do not need to arrive as AI engineers. They do need a real problem, willingness to learn, and the ability to participate consistently. Selected applicants receive the schedule, delivery format, expectations, and next steps by email.

Who may applyIndividuals, workers, small-business owners, nonprofit and community professionals, and other people with a practical use case.
Technical experiencePrior technical experience is not required for every pathway. Project scope is matched to readiness and available support.
Delivery formatCohorts may be in person, virtual, or hybrid. The format and dates are confirmed before enrollment.
Expected commitmentParticipants should attend consistently, complete project work, document decisions, and present what they learned.
EquipmentAccess to a laptop is generally expected. Applicants may identify access or accommodation needs during selection.
SelectionSelection considers the clarity of the need, readiness to participate, fit with available support, and responsible project scope.
CostAny cost, sponsorship, scholarship, or no-cost participation terms are communicated for each cohort before enrollment.
Data and safetyProjects involving sensitive, regulated, or high-risk information require additional review and may be narrowed or declined.
